# Geneva Video Style Guide

**Reverse-engineered from four OutdoorBoys-family Instagram reels (Luke Nichols, Alaska winter camping).**
Method: frame-by-frame extraction (per-shot in/out frames), automated cut detection, audio spectrogram + loudness analysis. Narration for Video 3 reconstructed from its burned-in captions. Speech transcription for the other three was not possible in this environment, so their narration is characterized (presence, energy, rhythm) rather than quoted.

All four videos: **360×640 delivered, 9:16 vertical, hard cuts only, zero music in the body content.**

## VIDEO 1 — "4 Days Camping in Alaska at -23 Degrees" (18.99s, 29.97fps)

### 1. Overall Vibe & Genre
- **Mood:** Triumphant, energetic, "you won't believe what we did" highlight-reel energy. Zero dread despite -23°; the cold is presented as a playground.
- **Genre:** Adventure-vlog **trailer**. This is not a self-contained story — it's a movie-trailer-style compression of a multi-day long-form video: one talking-head setup, then a rapid-fire montage of the seven coolest physical moments.
- Emotional tone: infectious competence. The subject is visibly comfortable doing hard things in a brutal place, and the edit celebrates it.

### 2. Structure & Pacing
- **Exact length:** 18.99s. Content: 0:00–13.9s. Branded logo end card: 13.9–19.0s (5.1s!).
- **Hook (0:00–1.8s):** First frame is already mid-sentence — a handheld selfie of the subject talking directly to camera with a **rifle slung over his shoulder**, low golden sun on his face, snow-covered mountain bowl behind him. No title, no logo, no dead air. The attention-grab is three simultaneous signals: direct eye contact + speech already in progress + an unusual prop (gun) in a spectacular place.
- **Segmentation:** 10 shots. Cuts at 1.8 / 3.3 / 5.2 / 6.9 / 8.3 / 9.0 / 9.9 / 11.6 / 13.9s. Average shot in the body: **~1.55s**, with two sub-second stingers (S6 rifle aim 0.7s, S7 ice auger 0.9s).
- **Rhythm:** One long-ish anchor shot (talking head), then accelerating montage — shots get shorter toward the middle (0.7–0.9s at 8.3–9.9s) then relax slightly for the final two process shots. Feels like a drum roll.
- **Ending:** At 13.9s the content just *stops* mid-energy and smash-cuts to the channel ritual: an axe strikes a tree stump and the orange "OUTDOOR BOYS" circular logo is stamped into the wood, then fades to black. The final impact moment is brand identity, not story resolution.

### 3. Camera & Cinematography
- **Arm-extended handheld selfie** is the signature: S1 (0–1.8s) talking head, S2 (1.8–3.3s) selfie while riding a snowmachine through rime-frosted trees, S4 (5.2–6.9s) selfie close-up with the sun directly behind his head producing a natural flare.
- **Static third-person wides** shot from a tripod/ground placement: S3 tree-felling, S5 kneeling at the hot tent, S9 assembling tent poles.
- **Action toward the lens:** at ~5.0s (S3 out) a snow-laden spruce falls *toward* the camera and the frame whites out in a snow explosion — the camera is placed where the action will physically reach it.
- **Backlit hero shot:** S8 (9.9–11.6s) is shot straight into the low sun with visible lens streaks while he heaves a snowmachine out of deep snow — silhouette + snow spray + flare.
- Framing: subject fills 40–60% of frame in selfies (face upper third, landscape behind); in wides he's small against big terrain. Everything is imperfect, slightly tilted, alive. No gimbal, no drone, no orbit.
- Depth of field: deep focus throughout (phone/action-cam look). No rack focus, no shallow bokeh.

### 4. Visual Style & Aesthetics
- **Palette:** classic blue/orange complementary — deep blue sky, white-blue snow, golden sunlight on skin, olive/khaki/brown wardrobe as the single earthy accent. Saturation is natural-high (crisp phone HDR look), contrast moderate.
- **Lighting:** 100% natural. Golden-hour low sun exploited constantly — three of ten shots deliberately shoot into or across the sun for flares and rim light. One shot (S9 tent build) is flat overcast, which reads as "later, colder."
- **Texture:** clean digital smartphone/action-cam. No grain, no LUT stylization, no vignettes. Compression artifacts and lens flares are the only "texture."
- **VFX:** none in the body. The only post-processed element is the animated logo end card.

### 5. Subject / Character Presentation
- Introduced **face-first, talking, mid-task** — never a slow reveal. Within 1 second you know: who (friendly guy in a parka), where (frozen mountains), and that he has something to tell *you*.
- Body language: constant purposeful physical work — riding, felling, drilling, hauling, building. Never posing. Effort is visible (S9: he's stripped down to a shirt at -23° and the shirt is frosted white — a physical storytelling detail that does the job of a paragraph of narration).
- Relationship to environment: mastery + playfulness. The environment is huge and hostile; he treats it like a workshop.
- Recurring motif: **tools in hand** (rifle, chainsaw-felled tree, auger, shovel, tent poles) — every shot has an object being *used*.

### 6. Editing & Transitions
- **Hard cuts only.** No dissolves, wipes, speed ramps, or freeze frames in the body. One fade-to-black at the very end of the logo card.
- Cuts land on action beats (tree starts falling → cut as it lands; auger bites ice → cut).
- **No text overlays at all in the body.** No captions, no title card. The talking head carries the verbal load.

### 7. Sound Design & Music
- Spectrogram shows **continuous energetic narration for the full 0–13.9s** — he talks over the entire montage, no gaps. Mean level -21 dB, peaks -3.4 dB (loud, present voice).
- **Zero music in the content.** Natural production sound underneath (engine, snow, tools).
- Music/SFX appears **only under the logo card** (14s+): an axe "thunk" and a short jingle texture.
- Audio rhythm = speech rhythm; cuts sit on his phrase boundaries.

### 8. What makes it scroll-stopping
- Frame 1 is a human face making eye contact and already mid-word — the brain treats it as being personally addressed.
- The rifle: an unexplained, mildly alarming prop that demands "why?"
- A new spectacular image every ~1.5s, each one physically bigger than a feed post deserves (tree crashing at the lens, sun-flared rescue heave).
- The -23° claim in the title + visible proof (frosted shirt, steaming breath) = instant "this is real" credibility.

## VIDEO 2 — "Camping in the Deepest Snow in Alaska" (17.51s, 29.97fps)

### 1. Overall Vibe & Genre
- **Mood:** Same triumphant trailer energy as Video 1, slightly more "epic scale" flavored — summits, waterfalls, sea-and-islands vistas.
- **Genre:** Adventure-vlog trailer / highlight montage. Identical editorial family to Video 1 (same creator-edited format, same end ritual).

### 2. Structure & Pacing
- **Exact length:** 17.51s. Content: 0–12.5s. Logo end card: 12.5–17.5s (5.0s).
- **Hook (0:00–1.6s):** Selfie on a rimed summit; he **points his index finger up, then directly AT the lens, then down into a deep ice canyon beside him** — all while talking, with the ocean and forested islands thousands of feet below. The pointing gesture is a command: "you — look at this."
- **Segmentation:** 8 shots. Cuts at 1.6 / 3.5 / 6.1 / 7.4 / 8.8 / 10.6 / 12.5s. Average body shot: **~1.8s**.
- **Rhythm:** Alternates effort and reward: balance-walk in front of a waterfall (S2) → strained climb close-up (S3, 2.6s — the longest, held because the face sells the effort) → gasping crawl over the cornice (S4, 1.3s) → serene ski glide (S5) → the payoff reveal (S6).
- **Ending / payoff:** S6 (8.8–10.6s) is the title's proof: an elevated angle looking **down into a hand-dug snow trench with cut block walls** (a snow fort), him working inside it, golden sunset over the sea on the horizon. Then S7: he walks toward the retreating camera, sunburst behind his head, grinning — a "come with me" exit. Then the axe-stamp logo card.

### 3. Camera & Cinematography
- Same language as V1: arm-extended selfies (S1 summit, S3 climbing, S4 crawling), static tripod wides (S2 waterfall, S5 ski traverse), one elevated overhead-ish angle (S6 trench), one retreating "follow me" pull-back (S7).
- **Extreme-close effort shot:** S4 fills the frame with his straining face and giant backpack as he crawls over a lip — lens inches from his glasses. Discomfort presented at intimate distance.
- Composition: S5 is a masterclass in negative space — skier small in upper third, ~60% of frame is untouched white snow. Scale contrast (tiny human / huge world) alternated with face-filling close-ups.
- Deep focus, no stabilization polish; the camera breathes with him.

### 4. Visual Style & Aesthetics
- Palette: white/ice-blue base, camo-olive wardrobe, warm gold injected twice (sunset behind the trench at 8.8–10.6s; sunburst behind his head at 10.6–12.5s).
- Lighting: natural; deliberately shoots into the sun for the two "reward" shots — warmth = achievement in this grammar.
- Texture: clean phone digital, mild HDR, no grade stylization, no effects.

### 5. Subject / Character Presentation
- Introduced pointing at the viewer mid-sentence — maximum direct address.
- Body language arc within 12 seconds: pointing (confidence) → wobbling on rocks (risk) → grimacing climb (effort) → gliding (grace) → digging (industry) → grinning walk-off (victory).
- Environment relationship: the terrain is an obstacle course he's beating; the snow trench shows him *reshaping* the environment — the deepest-snow claim made physical.
- Motif: the enormous backpack in every non-selfie shot — visual shorthand for self-sufficiency.

### 6. Editing & Transitions
- Hard cuts only, on narration phrases and action apexes. No text overlays in the body. Logo card with fade at the end.

### 7. Sound Design & Music
- Continuous narration 0–12.5s (spectrogram: unbroken speech formants), **no music**; natural ambience (waterfall, wind) under the voice. End-card jingle only after 12.9s. Mean -21.3 dB.

### 8. What makes it scroll-stopping
- The finger pointed at the lens in the first 0.5s — a startlingly personal gesture.
- Vertigo backdrop (summit + sea far below) visible before the first cut.
- Effort-face close-ups make you *feel* the climb in your chest.
- The trench-fort reveal pays off the title claim visually — the promise/proof loop closes inside 11 seconds.

## VIDEO 3 — "Dropped off in Alaska's Wilderness — The Train to Nowhere" (58.86s, 24fps)

### 1. Overall Vibe & Genre
- **Mood:** Survival mini-drama with warm, self-deprecating charm. Real stakes (alone, dusk approaching, deep snow) but narrated like a friendly letter.
- **Genre:** **Captioned micro-documentary / one-man survival story.** This is the "clips" format: fast-cut, wall-to-wall narration, karaoke captions, engineered for sound-off viewing. Different editorial family than V1/V2 (no logo card, caption-driven).

### 2. Structure & Pacing
- **Exact length:** 58.86s. No end card — ends on a cliffhanger.
- **Hook (0:00–1.4s):** Shot 1 (0–0.7s) is a **POV looking down at his own legs buried thigh-deep in snow**, caption "WELL GUYS, THAT" already on screen; shot 2 (0.7–1.4s) cuts to a locomotive receding down snowy tracks — "RIGHT THERE WAS." In 1.4 seconds you have a mystery (why is he alone?), a vehicle abandoning him, and a voice mid-story.
- **Segmentation:** **61 shots, average 0.96s** — many are 0.4–0.8s micro-shots. By far the fastest cutting of the four.
- **Rhythm:** Cut on nearly every narration phrase. The pacing never sits still, but the *story* is calm — fast form, slow content. Complete day/night/morning arc: dropped off (0–7s) → hike + 600-ft climb (7–23s) → dusk, "I need to make camp" (23–30s) → firewood, fire, curry-rice dinner at night (30–40s) → bed on lumpy ground (40–47s) → morning, fresh snow, twiggy oatmeal (47–53s) → cliffhanger (53.7–58.9s).
- **Ending / final impact:** The single longest shot in the video (S61, ~5.2s — 5× the average, a deliberate brake) is him walking through dark morning forest: "I just realized… without my…" — **the reel ends mid-sentence.** A pure open loop driving viewers to the long-form video / next clip.
- Reconstructed narration (from captions): *"Well guys, that right there was the Hurricane Train and it's just [dropped me] in the middle of nowhere here in the interior of Alaska… to hike my way back to civilization… a path through these woods. All right guys, first major [obstacle] — I gotta get up this 600-foot bench… I'm in the wrong place… climbing… get down… not having to break trail is [nice]… Well, the sun set and I'm [in] twilight. I need to make camp. All right… it is getting cold. I need to start finding some firewood… I think my [first] business is get some dinner — I got some rice cooking up [with curry sauce] and I'm looking forward to that. I'm really tired now that I got dinner in me… [thinking] about my bed — the ground here is really lumpy… I'm gonna see [you in] the morning. [It] snowed a bit last night… some instant [oatmeal] with little bits of alder twigs in it… I just realized… without my…"*

### 3. Camera & Cinematography
- Three alternating positions, all handheld: **(a)** arm-extended selfie while walking/snowshoeing (talking, breath visible), **(b)** POV down at his own feet/hands/gear (snowshoe strapping at 2.4–2.8s, glove punching into snow at 7.7–8.8s), **(c)** quick third-person statics (camera set on the sled/snow: train tracks receding at 6.2–7.0s, gear wide).
- Night shots are lit **only by his headlamp and the fire** — the lamp beam becomes the framing device (pools of light in blackness, 23–47s).
- Framing is rough and truthful: horizons tilt, his head crops out of frame at 10.3s, snow hits the lens. This roughness is a *feature* — it reads as unfaked.
- No drone, no gimbal, no depth-of-field games.

### 4. Visual Style & Aesthetics
- **Light IS the story clock:** flat grey overcast (0–20s) → blue twilight (20–24s) → black night with headlamp + orange firelight (24–47s) → grey-blue dawn (47–59s). You can read the plot with the sound off purely from color temperature.
- Palette: desaturated grey-white-brown daytime; at night, a two-color scheme of **orange flame vs. blue-black snow** (30.2–31.1s: flames fill half the frame while he stands behind in headlamp).
- Texture: gritty low-light phone footage at night, visible noise — never cleaned up.
- The only "effect" is the caption system.

### 5. Subject / Character Presentation
- Introduced by his *predicament* before his face (legs-in-snow POV first, face at ~7s). The character is "a voice in trouble" for the first beats.
- Body language: perpetual forward motion; struggles shown honestly (post-holing, kneeling, dragging the sled) but narrated cheerfully — the charm is the gap between situation and tone.
- Environment: adversary-turned-home. He converts wilderness into camp (fire, dinner, bed) in real time.
- Motifs: headlamp band on his forehead (night identity), the green spoon / blue cup (recurring props), gear with visible brand wear.

### 6. Editing & Transitions
- Hard cuts, ~1 per second, timed to narration phrases (the caption chunk changes exactly on the cut in most cases).
- **Karaoke captions, the signature graphic:** ALL-CAPS, chunky bold sans-serif, white fill with heavy black outline, 2–4 words per screen, positioned center-frame at ~65% height, **current word highlighted YELLOW** ("GUYS," "HURRICANE," "TRAIN," "NOWHERE," "CIVILIZATION," "TWILIGHT," "FIREWOOD"). The highlighted words alone tell the story for muted viewers.
- No speed ramps or freeze frames detectable; energy comes entirely from cut rate.

### 7. Sound Design & Music
- Spectrogram: **unbroken narration across all 59 seconds** — the densest voice track of the four. No music anywhere.
- Under the voice: real foley (crunching snow, saw, fire crackle, pot sounds) at every location change.
- The audio is a podcast; the video is its illustration; the captions are its subtitles. Three redundant layers = works muted, works audio-only, works both.

### 8. What makes it scroll-stopping
- 0.0s: a body in trouble (legs swallowed by snow) + a caption already talking to "GUYS."
- 0.7s: a train abandoning the narrator — instant premise, no setup.
- Yellow-flash keywords let a muted, half-attentive viewer follow a full narrative arc.
- Day→night→dawn compression gives a feature-film-sized arc in one minute.
- The mid-sentence cliffhanger ending weaponizes the Zeigarnik effect (unfinished business is remembered).

## VIDEO 4 — "Deep Snow Shelter in Alaska" (59.98s, 30fps)

### 1. Overall Vibe & Genre
- **Mood:** Meditative, competent, quietly satisfying. The pleasure is watching raw snow become architecture.
- **Genre:** **Process documentary / build video** (ASMR-adjacent). The slowest and most cinematic of the four — closer to primitive-building content than to vlog trailers.

### 2. Structure & Pacing
- **Exact length:** 59.98s. Content 0–55.6s; "#Outdoor Boys — Go Subscribe" card (white text + red subscribe button on black) 55.6–60s, with the last ~2s fully silent.
- **Hook (0:00–6.3s):** One unbroken static wide — he stands on skis and **plunges an avalanche probe its full length into the snowpack**, a fog bank and rime-ghost forest behind him. No face close-up, no caption yet. The hook is a single strange, legible action: "the snow here is deeper than the stick."
- **Segmentation:** 17 shots, **average 3.5s** — 3–4× slower than V1/V2, and the opener holds 6.3s. Longest shots: S1 6.3s, S13 6.1s (39.2–45.4s, night interior).
- **Rhythm:** Patient build montage. Each shot is one construction stage: probe → drop pack → explain to camera ("(8.5 ft / 2.59 m)" caption at 12.3s) → dig → trench deepens → ground vegetation revealed at the bottom (proof of depth) → arched doorway carved → blocks sawn → night interior → stove fire → boiling pot → cozy grin.
- **Ending:** the payoff is *inhabitation* — him inside the finished glowing shelter at night with a twig stove burning (45.4–55.6s), then the subscribe card. Final content image: his smiling face lit warm inside the blue snow cave, wisps of smoke curling past.

### 3. Camera & Cinematography
- Predominantly **fixed tripod setups repositioned between stages** — the discipline of a build video: wide from behind (throwing shovel-loads at 16.8–22.4s), reverse angle over the deepening wall, selfie close-up for the explanation beat (9.3–15.3s).
- **Signature angle: the buried camera.** At 23.7–27.4s the camera sits at the *bottom of the trench* looking up as snow falls onto the lens; later angles shoot up at him against the sky from inside the hole, and through the carved doorway arch (30.9–32.4s) using the arch as a natural frame-within-frame.
- Night interior: camera inside the shelter, lit by headlamp + stove flame only.
- Deep focus throughout; scale communicated by cutting between "him small on the white field" and "walls towering over the lens."

### 4. Visual Style & Aesthetics
- Daytime palette: near-monochrome **white-on-white with ice-blue shadows**, grey fog band, his grey fleece + khaki pants barely separating from the snow; teal shovel and red probe are the only saturated objects (tool = color accent).
- Night palette: **blue snow walls + orange flame** — the same two-color night scheme as V3, but composed more carefully (flame lower-third, blue vault above).
- Sunlight appears mid-video (22.4–33.7s block-carving in full sun with blue sky) making the structure glow — the "reward light" again.
- Texture: noticeably higher image quality than V1–V3 (newer capture), still natural — no grade, no grain overlay, no effects.

### 5. Subject / Character Presentation
- Introduced as a **silhouette of competence** — full-body, mid-task, face not shown until 9.3s. The build is the protagonist; he is its servant.
- Body language: rhythmic, unhurried labor — shovel arcs, saw strokes. He strips to a fleece while digging (effort signal), adds a shell at night (time/temperature signal).
- Environment relationship: literally *entering* the landscape — he ends the video inside the snowpack he measured at the start. Perfect circular structure: probe goes into the snow at 0:03; he is inside the snow at 0:50.
- Motifs: tools (probe, shovel, snow saw, twig stove), the arched doorway, steam/smoke against blue.

### 6. Editing & Transitions
- Hard cuts on completed actions (a shovel-load lands → cut). No transitions, no ramps.
- Text: one small, quiet caption — white parenthetical "(8.5 ft / 2.59 m)" lower-left at 12.3s. Data delivered without shouting. End card is plain white-on-black + red subscribe button.

### 7. Sound Design & Music
- **No music.** Narration is intermittent here (unlike V1–V3) — he talks in the explanation beats and lets long stretches carry on pure foley: shovel scrape, saw rasp, stove roar, fire crackle (strong broadband energy on the spectrogram at 35–52s).
- Silence used deliberately: the final 2s of the card are dead silent — a full stop.
- The foley is the ASMR contract: real, close-mic'd work sounds.

### 8. What makes it scroll-stopping
- The probe vanishing into bottomless snow in the first seconds — a one-gesture "wait, how deep?" question.
- Transformation promise: viewers stay to see raw material become shelter (same psychology as primitive-building megahits).
- The buried-camera angle delivers a perspective a phone-scroller has never occupied.
- The warm-light-inside-cold-world final image is a primal comfort payoff.

# SYNTHESIZED STYLE BIBLE FOR GENEVA VIDEOS

## The shared DNA (what all four do)

1. **Frame 1 is mid-action, mid-sentence, mid-problem.** No logos, titles, or establishing dead air. The video behaves as if you walked in on something already happening.
2. **One character, alone, talking to *you*.** The camera is the second character — an arm-length friend being addressed directly. Humans other than the subject never appear.
3. **Structure = Claim → Physical Proof → Payoff → Ritual.** A verbal/visual promise in the first 2s, escalating physical process in the middle, a payoff image that closes the promise, then a fixed channel ritual (logo stamp) or an open loop (cliffhanger).
4. **Cold world, one warm light.** Blue/white/grey environments with exactly one warm element (golden sun, orange fire) reserved for reward moments.
5. **No music. Ever. In the body.** Voice + real foley carry 100% of the audio. Music exists only inside the branded end card.
6. **Hard cuts only, on phrase or action boundaries.** Zero dissolves, ramps, or transition effects across all 96 shots analyzed.
7. **Effort is the entertainment.** Strained faces, frosted shirts, tipping balance, lumpy ground. Discomfort shown honestly, narrated cheerfully.
8. **Scale whiplash:** tiny-figure-in-huge-landscape wides alternate with lens-distance face/hand close-ups.
9. **Light is the clock.** Time passing is told through color temperature (grey → gold → blue dusk → orange-on-black night → grey dawn), never through text like "later."
10. **Recurring props = authored world.** Tools, the headlamp, the giant pack, the green spoon. Objects persist and get used, not displayed.

## Recommended length & structure template (Geneva episodes, 15s)

The V1/V2 trailer template maps 1:1 onto a 15s Seedance episode. The V3/V4 formats are the model for how a future 30–60s format would work; steal their devices (captions, light-clock, buried camera) now.

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **HOOK** | 0.0–2.0s | Geneva mid-gesture, mid-"sentence" (captions already on screen), facing camera at arm's length, with one odd prop/situation visible. Chest glow state readable. |
| **ESCALATION** | 2.0–10.5s | 4–5 shots × ~1.7s. Alternate selfie-cam / static wide / POV-of-hands. Each shot = one physical step of the attempt, difficulty rising. |
| **PAYOFF** | 10.5–13.5s | The longest shot of the video (2.5–3s — the "brake," like V3's 5.2s closer). The reveal, the small victory, or the beautiful failure. Warm light or glow-surge belongs HERE and only here. |
| **RITUAL / LOOP** | 13.5–15.0s | Either the fixed channel sign-off beat (Geneva's equivalent of the axe-stamp — same 1.2s animation every episode), or a mid-sentence cliffhanger caption for multi-part stories. Never both. |

Wander clips (8–10s): use the V4 skeleton instead — one static wide of a single strange legible action (the "probe plunge"), one close-up reaction, one payoff. 3 shots, no ritual card needed.

## Camera language

- **Default A-camera: Geneva's arm-extended selfie.** Slight sway, imperfect tilt, his face upper-third, world behind. This is the direct-address channel voice and satisfies the anti-slop handheld rule.
- **B-camera: static ground/tripod wide**, placed *where the action will arrive* (tree falls at it, snow gets thrown onto it, Geneva walks into it). One per video minimum.
- **C-camera: POV of his own hands/feet/tendrils** interacting with the thing (V3's snowshoe strapping, glove-in-snow).
- **Signature steal — the buried camera** (V4): put the lens inside the hole / under the object / at the bottom of whatever Geneva is digging into, looking up. Instantly premium, rarely seen in feeds.
- Frame-within-frame reveals (V4's carved doorway arch) for payoff shots.
- **Never:** drone orbits, gimbal glides, rack focus, slow-mo. Deep focus everywhere. If a shot looks professionally stabilized, it's off-brand.

## Color + lighting recipe

- Base world: **cold and desaturated** — blue-white snow-country, grey overcast, muted earth-tone environments. Geneva's grey skin belongs to this palette the way Luke's camo does.
- **Geneva's red glow is the campfire.** In this grammar, warm light = reward/comfort/achievement. Ration it: dim ember-state during setup and struggle, full glow-surge only at the payoff beat. A glow that's always on means nothing.
- One saturated *tool* accent per video (their teal shovel / red probe) — a single colorful object Geneva uses.
- Prefer one deliberate into-the-light shot per episode (sun or glow behind the head, natural flare) — it's the family's hero-shot signature.
- Time-of-day arcs even in 15s: pick two adjacent light states (gold → dusk, dusk → dark-with-glow) and let the cut between them mark story time.
- Grade: clean, natural, phone-real. No LUT looks, no grain overlays, no vignettes.

## Editing rhythm

- Hard cuts only. Cut on completed micro-actions or caption-phrase boundaries.
- Body shots 1.4–2.0s; you may drop one 0.7s stinger mid-escalation (V1's rifle-aim) for a drum-roll feel.
- **The payoff shot must be 1.5–3× longer than the average shot** — the brake before impact is what makes fast cutting feel intentional rather than random.
- End card discipline: OutdoorBoys spends 5s of a 19s reel on brand ritual; at 15s Geneva can afford **max 1.5s** — same idea, compressed. Identical animation every time, so it reads as a signature, not an outro.

## Text & captions (the sound-off answer)

- Adopt V3's karaoke captions as the standard for episodes: ALL-CAPS chunky bold sans, white with heavy black outline, 2–4 words per screen, centered at ~65% frame height, **one keyword per phrase highlighted** (Geneva's accent should be his red, not yellow — glow-colored keywords tie text to character).
- Written in Geneva's first-person voice, warm and literal ("WELL FRIENDS, THAT RIGHT THERE WAS THE 6:15 BUS").
- The highlighted keywords alone must tell the story — test by reading only them.
- V4's alternative for quiet data beats: a small white parenthetical "(8.5 ft / 2.59 m)"-style caption for measurements/facts Geneva records. Perfect for his field-log persona.
- Never both caption systems in the same shot.

## Presenting Geneva like the family presents its subject

- **Face/eyes first, already talking (captioning) to camera** — the viewer must be personally addressed within 1s (episodes), or shown one strange legible full-body action (wander clips, V4-style).
- Give him a **prop over the shoulder** in the hook — the "rifle trick": an unexplained carried object that makes the viewer ask why.
- **Point at the lens.** V2's finger-point is the single most aggressive scroll-stopper observed; Geneva pointing a tendril at the viewer, then at the problem, is a ready-made hook.
- Show effort honestly: late catches, off-balance arms, the alien equivalent of the frosted shirt (frost on tendrils? glow dimming with exhaustion?). Struggle → cheerful caption is the family's charm engine.
- Let him **reshape the environment** (dig, build, assemble, convert) — the payoff is always a changed world, not just a reaction. This is what separates these videos from pure reaction slop.
- Circular structure when possible (V4: probe enters snow at 0:03, he's inside the snow at 0:50). Plant the payoff object in the hook.

## Hook formulas (pick one per video)

1. **Mid-sentence + odd prop:** selfie, captions mid-phrase, unexplained object on shoulder. (V1)
2. **The point:** finger/tendril at lens → sweep to the problem beside him. (V2)
3. **Body in trouble, face withheld:** POV down at his own limbs stuck/failing + "WELL FRIENDS, THAT…" — face arrives 5+ seconds later. (V3)
4. **One strange legible action, no words:** static wide, a single gesture that poses a question ("the probe vanishes"). Reserve for wander clips. (V4)
5. **The abandoning vehicle:** something leaves Geneva behind in frame 2 (bus pulls away, elevator closes) — instant premise. (V3's train)

## Do / Avoid

**DO**
- Start every video mid-action AND mid-caption; delete your first planned shot — the second one is usually the real hook.
- Keep one long "brake" shot right before the end; let the payoff breathe.
- Use real-sounding foley for every action even though viewers are mostly muted — the 20% with sound get the ASMR layer (and captions serve the rest).
- Repeat the identical sign-off ritual every episode until it's a brand reflex.
- Shoot into the light once per video; embrace the flare.
- Let weather/light state change across the video — it's the cheapest production value there is.
- End multi-part stories mid-sentence (V3's "without my…" is the strongest retention device in all four videos).

**AVOID**
- Music beds under content — the family's realism collapses the moment a track starts. (Also: never name commercial tracks in prompts.)
- Title cards, logos, or channel branding before the final beat.
- Dissolves, speed ramps, zoom transitions, freeze frames — 96 shots analyzed, zero of any of these.
- Perfectly stable, perfectly framed "cinematic" shots — a straight horizon three shots in a row reads as AI slop in this grammar.
- Constant glow — if Geneva's red light never changes state, it stops being information.
- Explaining with text what the image already shows; captions carry *voice*, not description.
- Spending more than 1.5s of a 15s runtime on the outro ritual.
